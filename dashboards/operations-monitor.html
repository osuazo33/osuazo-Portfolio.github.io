      About this dashboard

      Business problem

      An online store's operations team needs to know, during the day, whether orders are coming in at the usual pace, whether payments are going through, and which categories carry revenue, so they can react to payment incidents before customers complain.

      Objective

      One screen that answers "are orders flowing and getting paid right now?", compares each figure with the previous 30 minutes, points at the one thing that needs attention, and lets the user drill into the order rows behind every number.

      How the numbers are defined

      Orders
      Orders placed in the last 30 minutes, any status.
      Completed revenue
      Sum of order amounts whose payment completed. Pending, failed and refunded orders are excluded.
      Payment success
      Completed ÷ (completed + failed + refunded). Pending orders are left out of the denominator until they resolve.
      Average order value
      Completed revenue ÷ completed orders.
      Change
      Against the 30 minutes before, with every status evaluated now.

      What to look for

      • A drop in payment success with failures concentrated in one payment method points to a provider issue, not demand.
      • A rising open-order queue with steady order volume means confirmations are slow.
      • Revenue moving while order count is flat is a mix effect: check the category breakdown.

      Built with

      HTML, CSS and plain JavaScript; SVG charts drawn without a charting library; Fetch API with a timeout and a local fallback. Read the data model and chart code.

      Data sources

      Orders are simulated in your browser and are not real business results. Each order decides its outcome when it is created, so KPIs, charts, the table and notifications are all computed from the same rows and always agree.

      The product catalog (names, categories, prices) is requested once from the public DummyJSON API. If it cannot be reached, a local catalog with the same categories is used and the status line says so.

      Updates arrive by polling every few seconds, not by streaming: no free API used here pushes data.

      Limitations

      • Order volume, statuses and timings come from a random model, so no trend here reflects a real store.
      • History covers the last 70 minutes and restarts when the page reloads.
      • Notifications are demo events derived from the simulated rows.
